Mechanism · Article 05

The anatomy involved

Two kinds of muscle, three nerves, one reflex principle — the anatomy every theory of the condition has to be compatible with.

27 min read · 14 references

Contents

    Summary

    • The erectile tissue of the penis is smooth muscle: involuntary, autonomically controlled, and capable of sustained contraction at low energy cost.
    • The pelvic floor is skeletal muscle: a different tissue, with different innervation and different control. The two cannot be treated as one system.
    • Flaccidity is not a resting state. It is actively maintained by continuous sympathetic outflow that keeps cavernosal smooth muscle contracted.
    • Erection is a relaxation event, not a contraction event.
    • Somatic sensory input can reflexively alter autonomic output. That principle is the bridge between an injury and a persistent change in smooth muscle tone, and it is the subject of the next article.
    Established textbook physiology or replicated finding Hypothesis proposed, partially supported Speculative stated without direct evidence Reasoning inference from the physiology, not a finding

    Most disagreements about hard flaccid syndrome (HFS) in patient communities are not really disagreements about HFS. They are disagreements about anatomy, conducted by people who have been given no reason to know the anatomy. A man reads that his pelvic floor is tight and concludes that tight pelvic floor muscles are squeezing his penis into a hard state. Another reads that posture matters and starts correcting his sitting position. A third reads about constipation and concludes his gut is the origin. Each of these is a reasonable inference from incomplete information. None of them survives contact with the tissue types involved.

    This article builds the picture from the ground up. It contains no theory of HFS at all — that comes next. What it contains is the set of facts that any theory has to be compatible with, so that when the theory arrives you can evaluate it rather than take it on faith.

    1. Two kinds of muscle Established

    Start here, because almost everything else follows from it. The body contains muscle types that differ not by degree but by kind, and the two relevant here are smooth muscle and skeletal muscle.

    Smooth muscle contains actin and myosin, but those filaments are not arranged into sarcomeres, which is why it has no striped appearance under a microscope. It operates involuntarily. It is regulated by hormones, by neurotransmitters and by mechanical stimulation rather than by direct motor commands. Contraction proceeds through calcium binding calmodulin, which activates myosin light chain kinase to phosphorylate myosin and form cross-bridges. Its rate of adenosine triphosphate consumption is much lower than that of skeletal muscle, which allows it to sustain forceful contraction for long periods without fatiguing.3

    That last property deserves a moment. A skeletal muscle held in contraction becomes painful and then fails. Smooth muscle does not work that way. It is built to hold a state indefinitely at low metabolic cost.3 Any explanation for a penis that stays firm for months has to involve a tissue capable of doing that, and smooth muscle is capable of it.

    Skeletal muscle is the opposite in every respect that matters here. It is striated, it is under voluntary control, and it is activated through direct neuromuscular junction signaling from somatic motor nerves.3 The pelvic floor is made of it. The levator ani complex — pubococcygeus, puborectalis and iliococcygeus — together with the coccygeus, forms a musculofascial complex at the floor of the pelvis, composed of striated fibers, roughly two-thirds slow-twitch and the remainder fast-twitch.6

    Key points

    • Cavernosal erectile tissue is smooth muscle: involuntary, autonomically driven, able to hold contraction indefinitely without fatigue.
    • The pelvic floor is skeletal muscle: striated, somatically innervated, partly voluntary, and it fatigues.
    • You cannot deliberately contract or relax cavernosal smooth muscle. You can deliberately contract or relax the pelvic floor.

    2. Three nerves, two systems Established

    The nervous system supplying the pelvis divides into an autonomic part, which controls smooth muscle and glands without conscious involvement, and a somatic part, which controls skeletal muscle and carries conscious sensation. The autonomic part divides again into sympathetic and parasympathetic branches.1,2

    Three named nerves carry this traffic to and from the penis, and keeping them distinct is worth the effort, because the entire mechanistic discussion in the next article is conducted in their terms.

    Table 2 · The three nerves
    Nerve System Spinal origin Principal role
    Hypogastric nerve Autonomic — sympathetic Thoracolumbar Carries sympathetic outflow to the pelvic organs and erectile tissue; maintains the contracted, flaccid state1,2
    Pelvic nerve (pelvic splanchnic) Autonomic — parasympathetic Sacral Carries the outflow that produces smooth muscle relaxation and erection1,2
    Pudendal nerve Somatic Ventral rami of S2–S44 Sensation from the genitals and perineum; motor supply to the external sphincters and perineal muscles4

    The pudendal nerve gives off three main branches: the inferior rectal nerve, the perineal nerve, and the dorsal nerve of the penis, which supplies sensation from the shaft and glans.4 That last branch matters more than its size suggests, because it is the sensory line running along the top of the penis, and it is the structure most exposed to a stretching or bending load. Sources differ slightly on its composition: one describes it as solely cutaneous,4 while another describes it as primarily sensory with a secondary motor contribution to the urethral sphincter and to ejaculatory reflexes at spinal level.5 For present purposes its sensory role is what counts.

    The distinction to carry forward: the pudendal nerve is somatic and carries sensation. The hypogastric nerve is sympathetic and drives smooth muscle contraction. They are separate nerves in separate systems, entering and leaving the spinal cord at different levels. Any account of how an injury in pudendal territory could change smooth muscle tone must explain how a signal crosses from one system to the other. Section 6 explains how such crossings work in general; the next article applies it.

    Key points

    • Autonomic nerves control smooth muscle involuntarily; somatic nerves control skeletal muscle and carry conscious sensation.
    • The hypogastric nerve is the sympathetic route to the erectile tissue; the pelvic nerve is the parasympathetic route; the pudendal nerve is the somatic one.
    • The dorsal nerve of the penis is a branch of the pudendal nerve and carries sensation from the shaft and glans.

    3. What the penis is made of Established

    Three cylinders of erectile tissue run the length of the organ: the paired corpora cavernosa, which do the work of rigidity, and the corpus spongiosum, which surrounds the urethra and expands into the glans. The corpora cavernosa are enclosed by the tunica albuginea, a tough fibrous sheath. Inside them, the tissue is a sponge of vascular spaces called sinusoids, lined with endothelium and separated by trabeculae of smooth muscle. Blood arrives through the cavernous arteries and their branching helicine arteries, and leaves through venules that run beneath the tunica.1,2

    The functional point is that the smooth muscle is not a lining around the vessels. It is the substance of the erectile tissue itself. When people speak of the penis being contracted in HFS, the tissue in question is this trabecular smooth muscle, distributed throughout the corpora.2 There is no single muscle to relax and no tendon to release.

    Key points

    • The corpora cavernosa provide rigidity; the corpus spongiosum surrounds the urethra and forms the glans.
    • Erectile tissue is a network of blood-filled sinusoids separated by smooth muscle trabeculae, enclosed by the tunica albuginea.
    • The smooth muscle is the tissue itself, distributed throughout, not a discrete muscle.

    4. Flaccidity is an active state Established

    This is the single most useful fact in the article, and the one most often missing from patient understanding. A soft penis is not a penis doing nothing. It is a penis being actively held soft.

    In the flaccid state, cavernosal smooth muscle is maintained in tonic contraction by continuous sympathetic outflow. Noradrenaline released from sympathetic nerve terminals acts on α-adrenoceptors on the smooth muscle cells, keeping them contracted. Contracted trabecular muscle keeps the sinusoids collapsed and limits arterial inflow, so the organ stays small and soft.1,2

    Turn that around and the implication is immediate. If sympathetic outflow to the penis increases beyond its normal level and stays increased, the predicted result is a penis that is firmer than it should be at rest, with reduced blood flow through the tissue, and impaired ability to achieve the relaxation that erection requires. That prediction follows from established physiology alone, before any HFS-specific claim is made.1,2 Whether that is what occurs in HFS is the subject of the next article.

    Key points

    • Flaccidity is maintained by continuous sympathetic activity, not by absence of activity.
    • Noradrenaline acting on α-adrenoceptors keeps cavernosal smooth muscle contracted.
    • Excess sympathetic tone would predict a firm flaccid penis with reduced inflow and impaired erection.

    5. Erection is a relaxation event Established

    Because flaccidity is contraction, erection is its opposite. Parasympathetic activity, conveyed through the cavernous nerves, leads to release of nitric oxide, which raises cyclic guanosine monophosphate in the smooth muscle cells and causes them to relax.1,2 Relaxed trabeculae allow the sinusoids to open. The helicine arteries dilate and arterial inflow rises sharply. As the sinusoids fill and expand, they compress the venules running beneath the tunica albuginea, and outflow falls. That trapping mechanism, sometimes called veno-occlusion, is what sustains the erection rather than inflow alone.1

    Phosphodiesterase type 5 inhibitors act at the end of this chain by slowing the breakdown of cyclic guanosine monophosphate, which prolongs the relaxation signal.2 This is worth knowing because it explains why such drugs might partially help a condition of excess contraction: they push the same tissue in the opposite direction, without addressing whatever is driving the contraction.2 Reasoning

    One further stage involves skeletal muscle, and it is the legitimate role of the pelvic floor in erection. Contraction of the ischiocavernosus and bulbospongiosus muscles compresses the erectile bodies from outside during the rigid phase, raising intracavernosal pressure above what arterial inflow alone could produce.1 These are striated muscles supplied by the pudendal nerve.4,6 Note precisely what they do: they squeeze the erectile bodies from the outside during a brief phase. They do not set the resting tone of the smooth muscle inside.

    Key points

    • Erection requires smooth muscle relaxation driven by nitric oxide and cyclic GMP.
    • Filling sinusoids compress subtunical venules, trapping blood; this maintains rigidity.
    • PDE5 inhibitors prolong the relaxation signal at the end of that chain.
    • Pelvic floor muscles compress the erectile bodies externally during the rigid phase; they do not control internal resting tone.

    6. What the pelvic floor does and does not do Established

    The pelvic floor is genuinely involved in this clinical picture, which is exactly why its role gets overstated. In the largest patient survey, 85.3% of 143 respondents reported weak or overactive pelvic muscles.9,10 Hypertonic pelvic floor disorders are recognized causes of urinary symptoms, elimination problems and chronic pelvic pain,7 and defecatory dysfunction from paradoxical contraction or inadequate relaxation of these muscles during attempted defecation is a documented consequence of pelvic floor dysfunction.8 Pelvic floor physical therapy is the intervention most frequently cited as optimal by surveyed sexual medicine practitioners.11

    All of that is true and none of it establishes that skeletal muscle tension causes the hard flaccid state. The reason is structural. The hard flaccid state is a property of trabecular smooth muscle inside the corpora, which is under autonomic control and cannot be commanded by a somatic nerve.1,2,3 The pelvic floor is skeletal muscle under somatic control, sitting outside the erectile bodies.6 A tight pelvic floor can compress structures passing through it, can generate pain, and can disturb urinary and bowel function. Holding the smooth muscle of the corpora in contraction for months is not among the things it is built to do.

    There is an honest complication here, and it should not be hidden. A competing model of HFS proposes that an initial injury damages neurovascular structures at the base of the penis, that resulting distress drives sympathetic stimulation and prolonged pelvic floor muscle spasm, and that this spasm compresses neurovascular structures and obstructs venous outflow, producing the semi-rigid flaccid state.12 In that account the pelvic floor is closer to the center of the mechanism. Neither model has been demonstrated. The systematic review lists both as current hypotheses.10 Hypothesis

    The distinction that survives either way is the one worth keeping: pelvic floor findings may be a parallel consequence of the same driver, a contributor to pain and urinary symptoms, or part of the causal chain. They are not the same tissue as the one producing the defining sign, and treating them as interchangeable makes the condition harder to reason about.

    Key points

    • Pelvic floor dysfunction is reported by 85.3% of surveyed patients and explains much of the urinary, bowel and pain picture.
    • It cannot directly set the tone of cavernosal smooth muscle, which is autonomically controlled.
    • A competing hypothesis places pelvic floor spasm nearer the center of the mechanism; neither model is established.

    7. Reflexes, and how sensation changes autonomic output Established

    One principle remains, and it is the bridge between everything above and the theory in the next article.

    A reflex is a fixed circuit: a sensory nerve carries information into the spinal cord, the cord processes it without involving conscious thought, and an outgoing nerve produces a response. The knee-jerk is the familiar example, and it is entirely somatic — sensory in, motor out, skeletal muscle responds.

    Reflexes are not confined to that arrangement. Stimulation of somatic sensory nerves can produce reflex changes in sympathetic nerve activity, and therefore in the function of organs those sympathetic nerves supply. Both noxious and innocuous somatic stimulation have been shown to evoke such changes, demonstrated across the gastrointestinal tract, urinary bladder, adrenal medulla, lymphatic tissue, heart, and blood vessels of the brain and peripheral nerves. These responses may be organized segmentally, meaning that input at one spinal level affects output at related levels.13

    This class of reflex is called somato-visceral, or somato-autonomic: somatic sensory input, autonomic output. It is established physiology, studied for decades.13 The author of that review also cautioned that the complexity of the mechanisms warrants further systematic research before clinical application, which is a caution worth carrying forward.13

    Note what this principle permits and what it does not. It permits sensory input from somatic territory — pudendal territory, for instance — to alter sympathetic outflow, and therefore to alter smooth muscle tone in organs supplied by that outflow. That is the crossing between systems flagged in section two. It does not, by itself, establish that this occurs in the penis, or that it occurs in HFS, or that it persists after an injury. Those are separate claims requiring separate evidence.

    Key points

    • A reflex is sensory input producing an automatic output through the spinal cord, without conscious involvement.
    • Somato-visceral reflexes convert somatic sensory input into changed sympathetic output, demonstrated across many organ systems.
    • This principle makes a route from somatic injury to altered smooth muscle tone physiologically possible; it does not demonstrate that route in HFS.

    8. Common explanations that do not fit the anatomy

    With the parts in place, several widely repeated accounts can be assessed directly. None of the entries below is dismissed as absurd; each is a reasonable guess that does not fit the structure once the structure is known.

    Table 3 · Assessing common explanations
    Claim Assessment
    “Tight pelvic floor muscles are squeezing my penis hard” Wrong tissue. Pelvic floor is skeletal muscle outside the erectile bodies; the hard flaccid state is smooth muscle inside them, under autonomic control3,6. Pelvic floor tension can cause pain and urinary symptoms and often coexists7,9
    “My posture caused this” No published evidence links posture to HFS. Symptoms do vary with position — 73.0% improve lying down, 64.5% worsen standing9 — but positional variation of a symptom is not evidence that position caused the condition
    “Muscle imbalances are the root cause” No published evidence. The claim also faces the tissue problem above: skeletal muscle arrangement does not set autonomic tone in the corpora1,3
    “It started in my gut” Constipation is reported by patients9 and is a recognized feature of pelvic floor dysfunction7,8. Shared pelvic floor involvement explains co-occurrence without requiring the gut to be the origin
    “It’s a blood flow problem” Partly, but not as usually meant. Penile color Doppler ultrasound showed normal peak systolic velocity in 98% of reported cases10, so arterial supply is typically intact. Reduced flow through contracted tissue is a consequence of smooth muscle state, not a disease of the arteries1
    “If I relax my pelvic floor hard enough the hardness will resolve” You cannot voluntarily relax cavernosal smooth muscle, because it is not under voluntary control3. Pelvic floor down-training addresses a different tissue, and pelvic floor physical therapy attracted a mean satisfaction score of 3.5 ± 2.4 out of 10 among surveyed patients9

    Absence of published evidence is not proof that a claim is false. It means the claim has not been tested, and that it should not be treated as established while untested.

    Key points

    • Explanations invoking skeletal muscle, posture or alignment run into the tissue-type problem.
    • Co-occurring symptoms are better explained by shared pelvic involvement than by one causing another.
    • Arterial supply is normal in the large majority of reported cases.

    9. What this sets up

    Assemble the pieces. Cavernosal smooth muscle is involuntary tissue capable of holding contraction indefinitely without fatigue.3 Its contracted state is maintained by sympathetic outflow arriving through the hypogastric nerve.1,2 Sensation from the penis travels a separate somatic route through the dorsal nerve and the pudendal nerve.4,5 And somatic sensory input is capable, through reflex circuits in the spinal cord, of altering sympathetic output to viscera.13

    Those four facts are individually established and are not specific to HFS. Put together, they describe a physiologically available route: an injury in somatic sensory territory could, in principle, drive a sustained change in sympathetic outflow, which would hold cavernosal smooth muscle contracted, which would produce a penis that is firm at rest and resistant to erection.

    Whether that route is what actually happens in hard flaccid syndrome is a different question, and it is the one the next article addresses. Goldstein and Komisaruk have proposed exactly such a mechanism, naming it a pelvic/pudendal-hypogastric reflex.14 The next article sets out that proposal in full, examines the evidence offered for it, identifies what it explains and what it does not, and states plainly which parts remain untested.

    You now have what you need to judge it rather than accept it.
