Literature

An annotated index of the entire literature, year by year. Short enough to read in an afternoon, which is the most important fact about it.

38 min read · 21 entries

Contents

    Summary

    • Twenty-one items make up the literature on hard flaccid syndrome (HFS): full papers, conference abstracts, correspondence, one preprint and two expert consensus documents that mention it.
    • The first clinical description appeared in 2019. Before that, the condition existed only in patient communities.
    • Across every study, roughly 162 patients have been clinically assessed and described. A further 143 self-identified respondents were surveyed without clinical verification.
    • The largest single cohort is a conference abstract of 88 men that found entirely normal penile hemodynamics — a finding with more mechanistic weight than its format suggests.
    • No randomized controlled trial of any intervention has been published. One study now has a comparison group, but it is not matched — those controls were thirteen years older on average.

    1. What is included, and how it was assembled

    This index covers every publication located that addresses hard flaccid syndrome as its subject, plus two consensus documents that classify it within a broader category. It includes conference abstracts, correspondence and one preprint, which formal reviews often exclude. That is a deliberate choice: in a field this small, a conference abstract reporting 88 patients carries more information than several full papers reporting one each, and pretending otherwise would misrepresent what is known.

    The 2025 systematic review searched Medline/PubMed, Embase and the Cochrane Library for “hard flaccid” or “hard-flaccid” between 1 January 2018 and 13 April 2025, and identified eight studies meeting its inclusion criteria.1 That figure and the twenty-one items indexed here are not in conflict, and it is worth being clear which is which. Eight is the count of studies passing one review’s criteria within a fixed date range. Twenty-one is everything located that addresses the condition, in any format. This index is broader because its inclusion criteria are looser, not because it found hidden material.

    One convention needs stating. Several of these papers appeared online in one year and in a print issue the following year, so the same study is cited with different years in different places. Entries below are organized by the year of first appearance, with the print volume and pages given in the citation. Where the two differ, both are shown.

    2. 2019 — the condition enters the literature

    Hard flaccid syndrome: initial report of four cases
    Gul M, Towe M, Yafi FA, Serefoglu EC. Int J Impot Res. 2020;32(2):176–179 (online 2019). doi:10.1038/s41443-019-0133-z Peer-reviewed
    Four men aged 22 to 34, all with symptom onset after trauma during intercourse or vigorous masturbation. Reported penile sensory change (numbness or coldness), a semi-rigid penis in the flaccid state, reduced morning and nocturnal erections, and difficulty with erection, plus pain during ejaculation or urination and psychological symptoms ranging from mild anxiety to severe depression. Laboratory and imaging tests were inconclusive; phosphodiesterase type 5 inhibitors were ineffective.
    The foundation document. Every subsequent paper is measured against these four cases, and the trauma-onset pattern established here still frames the field. The authors noted that HFS had not been universally recognized by urologists — a statement that remains substantially true.
    A qualitative analysis of Internet forum discussions on hard flaccid syndrome
    Gul M, Huynh LM, El-Khatib FM, Yafi FA, Serefoglu EC. Int J Impot Res. 2020;32(5):503–509 (online 2019). doi:10.1038/s41443-019-0151-x Peer-reviewed
    Thematic analysis of 12 English-language forum discussions containing 6,150 comments, collected between September and November 2018. Four themes emerged: diagnostic concerns, inquiry into causes, seeking treatment support, and coping strategies. Common symptoms were penile semi-hardness in the flaccid state, sensory change, erectile dysfunction and emotional distress. Most cases were reported to begin after a traumatic event.
    Unusual and valuable. It is the only study documenting that patients described and named this condition before medicine did, and the four themes it identified are the four functions of a clinical service being performed by patients for each other. It also anchors the symptom picture to a period before any clinical description existed, which reduces the risk that later reports simply echo the first paper.
    Comment on “A qualitative analysis of Internet forum discussions on hard flaccid syndrome”
    Yachia D. Int J Impot Res. 2020;32:551–553 (online 2019). doi:10.1038/s41443-019-0192-1 Correspondence
    The author initially took the forum analysis to describe “non-erecting erection,” a congenital condition he had documented previously, then concluded on full review that HFS is a distinct acquired condition typically following vigorous masturbation or penile trauma during intercourse. He argued that rare erectile disorders remain underrecognized because they lack clear descriptions and proper nomenclature.
    Small but worth reading. It is the earliest published attempt to place HFS against an adjacent condition, and its point about nomenclature anticipates the classification questions that occupy the field seven years later.

    Key points

    • Three items in the founding year, all in the same journal, two from the same group.
    • The forum analysis documents that the condition was described by patients first.
    • The trauma-onset pattern was established immediately and has not shifted since.

    3. 2020 — the first synthesis

    Hard flaccid syndrome: state of current knowledge
    Abdessater M, Kanbar A, Akakpo W, Beley S. Basic Clin Androl. 2020;30:7. doi:10.1186/s12610-020-00105-5 Peer-reviewed
    A review describing a constantly semi-rigid penis in the flaccid state with baseline penile engorgement, sensory changes including numbness or coldness with decreased sensitivity particularly at the glans, a soft and cold glans during erection, reduced morning and nocturnal erections, penile and perineal pain during urination and ejaculation, reduced urinary flow, and psychological distress including anxiety, depression, decreased libido and insomnia. Symptoms worsen in the standing position. Triggers listed include vacuum device use, vigorous masturbation or sexual activity, jelqing, excessive squatting, marijuana use, bremelanotide exposure and blunt trauma to the base of the erect penis. Examination is described as unremarkable; imaging, Doppler and hormonal studies normal. Proposed mechanism: initial trauma damages neurovascular structures at the penile base, distress drives sympathetic stimulation, and prolonged pelvic floor spasm compresses neurovascular structures producing hypoxia and venous outflow obstruction.
    Still the most detailed symptom catalog available, and the source of several findings — the cold soft glans, baseline engorgement, worsening on standing — that later papers cite without adding to. Its pathophysiological model, centered on pelvic floor spasm, remains the principal competitor to the sympathetic reflex hypothesis, and the systematic review lists both as live.

    4. 2021 — a framework arrives from elsewhere

    ISSWSH consensus on persistent genital arousal disorder / genito-pelvic dysesthesia
    Goldstein I, Komisaruk BR, Pukall CF, et al. J Sex Med. 2021;18(4):665–697. doi:10.1016/j.jsxm.2021.01.172 Expert consensus
    A fourteen-author panel using a modified Delphi method broadened the nomenclature for persistent genital arousal disorder to include genito-pelvic dysesthesia, on the basis that patients’ symptoms extend beyond arousal sensations alone. It set out a diagnostic algorithm localizing symptoms to one of five anatomical regions: end organ, pelvis/perineum, cauda equina, spinal cord, and brain.
    Not an HFS paper, and it is here because everything that follows depends on it. The five-region algorithm later applied to HFS is this algorithm, and the eventual classification of HFS as a dysesthesia originates in this document. Reading the HFS literature without it makes the 2023 and 2026 material look more novel than it is.

    5. 2022 — the largest cohort, in the smallest format

    Exploring erectile hemodynamics in men complaining of hard-flaccid syndrome
    Sullivan J, Salter C, Flores J, Guhring P, Parker M, Mulhall J. MP21-20. J Urol. 2022;207(Suppl 5). doi:10.1097/JU.0000000000002554.20 Abstract
    Eighty-eight men with self-reported HFS underwent hemodynamic assessment. Mean symptom duration was 14 ± 20 months. All reported impaired erectile function and 50% were incapable of penetrative sexual relations. All had normal peak systolic velocity, with 2 of 88 showing abnormal end-diastolic velocity. Mean peak systolic velocity was 45 ± 35 cm/s and mean end-diastolic velocity 2 ± 11 cm/s. No patient had elevated echogenicity on B-mode scanning. The authors concluded that men with self-reported HFS had normal hemodynamics and an absence of fibrosis, suggesting no vascular or gross cavernosal smooth muscle structural change.
    The most mechanistically informative study in the corpus, and it is a conference abstract. Three implications follow. It is by a wide margin the largest clinically assessed HFS cohort, larger than every full paper combined. It establishes that the arterial supply and the veno-occlusive mechanism are intact in this population, which removes vascular disease as an explanation and forces attention onto smooth muscle tone and neural control. And the absence of fibrosis on B-mode in 88 men sits awkwardly beside the single later case reporting mild corporal fibrosis — a tension nobody has addressed, though B-mode echogenicity is a coarse measure and short symptom duration in this cohort may explain it.
    Successful treatment of hard flaccid syndrome: a case report
    Nico E, Rubin R, Trosch L. Abstract 203. J Sex Med. 2022;19(Suppl 1):S103. doi:10.1016/j.jsxm.2022.01.218 Abstract
    A 16-year-old (btcalvit) became symptom free after specialized pelvic floor physical therapy including exercises targeting abdominal and gluteal muscles. Prior circumcision for phimosis had not helped. The authors concluded that individualized therapy addressing impairments beyond the pelvic floor itself may be necessary.
    One of the youngest patients described anywhere in this literature, and one of very few complete resolutions reported. As a single unblinded case it cannot establish that the therapy caused the outcome, and a 16-year-old with short symptom duration is the profile in which spontaneous recovery is most plausible.

    Key points

    • The 88-man hemodynamic study is the largest cohort in the literature and was never published as a full paper.
    • It found normal arterial inflow, normal veno-occlusive function and no fibrosis.
    • That result pushes explanation toward smooth muscle tone and neural control rather than vascular disease.

    6. 2023 — a mechanism is proposed

    Hard flaccid syndrome proposed to be secondary to pathological activation of a pelvic/pudendal-hypogastric reflex
    Goldstein I, Komisaruk BR. AUANews. May 2023. auanews.net Society commentary
    Proposes that HFS results from pathological activation of a somato-visceral or viscero-visceral reflex, termed pelvic/pudendal-hypogastric, in which afferent input drives excess sympathetic activity through the hypogastric nerve to erectile smooth muscle, which remains contracted. The reflex is proposed to be triggerable at five anatomical regions. Support offered includes the observation that intracavernosal phentolamine temporarily resolves the hard flaccid state and induces erection.
    The most influential document in the field, and the one whose format is furthest from its influence. It appeared in a professional society news publication, not as peer-reviewed primary research, and it reports no sample size, dose, protocol, control or objective measure. Its central evidence is confounded: phentolamine produces erection in men without HFS, so an erection following injection cannot distinguish normalized resting tone from a drug working as intended. Despite this the reflex model now organizes most specialist thinking about the condition.
    Hard flaccid syndrome: a biopsychosocial management approach with emphasis on pain management, exercise therapy and education
    Billis E, Kontogiannis S, Tsounakos S, Konstantinidou E, Giannitsas K. Healthcare (Basel). 2023;11(20):2793. doi:10.3390/healthcare11202793 Peer-reviewed
    A case managed with a biopsychosocial strategy centered on pain management, therapeutic exercise including pelvic floor re-education, graded exposure to activity, and education. The authors describe HFS as an acquired condition of young men presenting with penile somatosensory disturbance, a semi-rigid flaccid penis without stimulation or desire, erectile dysfunction, perineal or penile pain, urinary symptoms and emotional distress, and suggest initial penile trauma causing minor nerve and vascular disturbance as the trigger.
    The only paper in the corpus written primarily from a physiotherapy and rehabilitation perspective rather than a urological one, and the source of the 85% improvement at three months figure cited in the systematic review. Its value is in describing a structured management protocol at a level of detail the urological case reports do not attempt. Its limitation is the same as theirs: one patient, no control, unblinded.

    7. 2024 — the field’s most productive year

    Hard flaccid syndrome symptoms, comorbidities, and self-reported efficacy and satisfaction of treatments: a cross-sectional survey
    Niedenfuehr J, Stevens DM. Int J Impot Res. 2025;37:458–464 (online 2024). doi:10.1038/s41443-024-00853-2 Peer-reviewed
    An open 42-question online survey run on Qualtrics between 9 May and 9 June 2023, using purposive and convenience sampling with recruitment through social media. 143 respondents, mean age 27.4 years. Change in penis shape or size 92.3%, rigid penis when flaccid 90.9%, psychological distress 89.5%, weak or overactive pelvic muscles 85.3%, numbness or sensory loss 74.8%, erectile difficulty 74.1%, decreased urinary stream 72.7%. Symptoms began after a specific incident in 58.0%. Lying down improved symptoms in 73.0% and stretching in 44.1%; masturbation worsened them in 75.9% and standing in 64.5%. Pudendal neuralgia was the most prevalent comorbidity at 16.9%. Treatment satisfaction on a 0–10 scale: PDE5 inhibitors 4.8 ± 2.6, pelvic floor physical therapy 3.5 ± 2.4, cognitive therapy 2.9 ± 2.4, diet and nutrition 2.8 ± 2.6, nerve pain medications 2.7 ± 2.6, anti-inflammatory medications 2.6 ± 2.1, muscle relaxants 2.5 ± 2.0, shockwave therapy 2.0 ± 2.4, nerve blocks 1.9 ± 1.5. The authors concluded that current therapies generally do not benefit most patients.
    The source of nearly every percentage quoted about this condition anywhere, including throughout this site. Its reach is a function of being the only study to quantify anything at scale. Its weaknesses should travel with its numbers: respondents were self-identified rather than clinically diagnosed, recruitment through social media selects for persistent and distressing presentations, and no control group was included. These are descriptions of a self-selected population, not prevalence estimates.
    Prevalence of lumbosacral annular tears in individuals with hard flaccid syndrome
    Goldstein I, Komisaruk BR, Goldstein SW, Yee A. Abstract 149. J Sex Med. 2024;21(Suppl 1):qdae001.140. doi:10.1093/jsxmed/qdae001.140 Abstract
    Twenty-one individuals with HFS recruited through an online support group, mean age 28 years (range 19–42), submitted lumbar MRI performed within the preceding year. A spine surgeon reviewed sagittal and axial views. Sixteen of 21 (76%) had surgically treatable annular tears, predominantly at L5–S1 and L4–L5, against a prevalence of 19% to 29% reported in asymptomatic populations in prior literature.
    The only attempt to test a specific prediction of the reflex model — that a cauda equina trigger should be detectable — and the field’s most discussed imaging finding. The limitations are heavy: congress abstract, n = 21, support-group recruitment, no concurrent matched control group, comparison to historical figures, single reader not reported as blinded. Annular tears are common in asymptomatic people, so base rate alone could account for a substantial share. Hypothesis-generating, not established.
    Hard-flaccid syndrome: a survey of sexual medicine practitioners’ knowledge and experience
    Gryzinski G, Hammad MM, Alzweri L, et al. Int J Impot Res. 2024;37(6):446–447. doi:10.1038/s41443-024-00917-3 — also presented as abstract 225, J Sex Med. 2024;21(Suppl 1):qdae001.215. doi:10.1093/jsxmed/qdae001.215 Peer-reviewed
    Of 37 attendees at the 2023 American Urological Association sexual medicine plenary, 36 completed a survey; 35 were urologists and one a mental health professional. 64% (23/36) had encountered a patient with HFS. Only 52% considered HFS a legitimate medical condition. 13% reported treatment success and 57% inconsistent results. Diagnosis relied predominantly on clinical history and patient self-report with minimal imaging; examinations were normal or inconsistent. Pelvic floor therapy was most frequently cited as optimal, by 11 respondents. The authors identified small sample size and survey methodology as the main limitations.
    The only measurement of what clinicians actually think, and the 52% figure is the one patients most need explained. It records a subspecialty audience three years after the first clinical description, working without diagnostic criteria or a confirmatory test. Because respondents self-selected into a sexual medicine session, the figures likely overstate familiarity in general practice rather than understate it.
    Successful treatment of hard flaccid syndrome with multimodal therapy: a case report study
    Yazar RO, Hammad MAM, Barham DW, Azad B, Yafi FA. Int J Impot Res. 2024;37(6):448–450. doi:10.1038/s41443-024-00955-x Peer-reviewed
    A 36-year-old with penile rigidity during flaccidity, diminished libido, chronic perineal and penile pain and erectile insufficiency, who had performed self-directed stretching exercises without benefit and with increased pain. Testosterone, prolactin and thyroid function were normal; penile Doppler showed mild corporal fibrosis with no other findings and no palpable plaque. Treated with tadalafil 5 mg daily, six sessions of low-intensity shockwave therapy, and physical therapy one to two times weekly over 10 to 12 weeks. Significant regression of complaints, with resolved penile rigidity, improved erection quality, and sustained relief at two-year follow-up without side effects.
    The longest follow-up in the literature, and the clearest documented instance of self-directed penile stretching preceding onset — the mechanism the field’s leading hypothesis assumes. Its mild corporal fibrosis is the only structural abnormality reported anywhere in this corpus, which makes it either an important signal or an outlier; the 88-man cohort found no fibrosis. As three interventions were given together, the report cannot attribute the outcome to any one of them.

    Key points

    • Four studies in one year, the field’s most productive.
    • The 143-respondent survey supplies nearly every percentage in circulation.
    • The annular tear abstract is the only test of a specific mechanistic prediction and is heavily limited.
    • Only 52% of surveyed specialists regarded HFS as a legitimate condition.

    8. 2025 — consolidation

    Hard-flaccid syndrome: a report of two cases
    Pang KH, Feng J, Zhang Y. Int J Impot Res. 2025;37:486–489. doi:10.1038/s41443-025-01058-x Peer-reviewed
    Two patients with persistent penile semi-rigidity in the flaccid state following distinct triggers — aggressive masturbation in one, skin injury in the other. Investigations were normal in both. Management included validated symptom instruments and psychotropic medication.
    Notable for documenting two different triggers producing the same presentation, which supports the idea that HFS is a final common pathway rather than one injury with one consequence. It also brought the condition into the literature from a Chinese center, widening the geographic base beyond Europe and North America.
    Hard-flaccid syndrome: a systematic review of aetiopathophysiology, clinical presentation and management
    Pang KH, Feng J, Zhang Y. Int J Impot Res. 2025;38:393–403. doi:10.1038/s41443-025-01118-2 Systematic review
    Searched Medline/PubMed, Embase and the Cochrane Library from 1 January 2018 to 13 April 2025 and identified eight eligible studies: two retrospective studies, one case series of four patients, and five case reports including one describing two patients. Patient ages 16 to 42. Penile color Doppler ultrasound showed normal peak systolic velocity in 98% of cases. Two pathophysiological hypotheses were identified — a trauma–inflammation cascade and the pelvic/pudendal-hypogastric reflex — and a possible penis–brain axis analogous to the gut–brain axis in irritable bowel syndrome was raised as requiring further investigation. Only 20% of patients achieved symptom resolution. No objective test assists diagnosis; diagnosis relies on subjective symptom review and exclusion. Heterogeneous reporting prevented quantitative meta-analysis, and the authors noted a high risk of bias in many included reports and called for expert consensus guidelines, standardized diagnostic criteria and further basic science.
    The reference point for the field. Its most useful output is not a conclusion but an inventory: eight studies meeting its criteria, no controlled trials, no diagnostic criteria, no confirmatory test, 20% resolution. Anyone claiming more certainty than that about HFS is claiming more than this review found. It also declines to adjudicate between the two competing mechanisms, which is the correct position given the evidence.
    The conundrum that is the hard flaccid syndrome
    Punjani N, Mulhall JP. J Sex Med. 2025;22(11):2007–2008. doi:10.1093/jsxmed/qdaf221 Editorial
    Observes that HFS first appeared in the literature in 2020 and remains inadequately studied, with 12 PubMed results against approximately 410,000 general web results. Notes that patient-driven online communities provide support while distributing unvetted information, that the condition primarily affects men in their twenties and thirties, that physician visits have increased since initial documentation, and that true prevalence remains unknown and likely underreported.
    Two pages, and it names the structural problem more precisely than anything else in the corpus: demand for explanation exceeds supply by roughly four orders of magnitude, and something fills that gap. Coming from a high-volume sexual medicine center, it also signals that the condition is being taken seriously by clinicians who see many patients.

    9. 2026 — classification, and the first objective measure

    (595) Forward-looking infrared (FLIR) for the objective diagnosis and monitoring of patients with hard flaccid syndrome
    Koontz K, La Rosa J, Neustein M, Goldstein S, Kim N, Goldstein I. J Sex Med. 2026;23(Suppl 4):qdag118.525. doi:10.1093/jsxmed/qdag118.525 Abstract
    Thermographic imaging of 250 images from 12 men with HFS (mean age 36.4 ± 11.1) and 38 without (mean age 49.4 ± 16.8), taken supine at the glans and mid-shaft in flaccid and erect states between July and October 2025. The flaccid glans was 88.2°F in the HFS group against 91.4°F in controls (p = 0.0003). Flaccid mid-shaft was 94.0°F against 94.6°F; erect glans 93.5°F against 94.6°F; erect mid-shaft 94.9°F against 95.7°F. In five treated patients the flaccid glans rose from 86.6°F to 89.9°F. The authors conclude that FLIR supplies an objective physiological measure for diagnosis and for monitoring treatment.
    The first objective measurement anyone has proposed for this condition, which is why it matters more than its format suggests. Every treatment outcome in the rest of this literature is self-reported; this is a number taken off an instrument. The limitations are the usual ones and are substantial: a congress abstract, 12 patients, and a control group thirteen years older on average, when skin temperature and perfusion both vary with age. Only one of the four measurements separated the groups, and the difference in that one was 3.2°F (1.8°C). It is a lead worth pursuing rather than a validated test.
    Multimodal rehabilitation improves erectile function and quality of life in men with hard flaccid syndrome
    Mendias CL, Awan TM. Preprints.org. Posted 14 May 2026. doi:10.20944/preprints202605.0986.v1 Preprint
    Retrospective case series of 32 men completing a protocol combining class IV laser therapy, dry needling, radial pressure wave shockwave therapy, therapeutic ultrasound, biofeedback training, manual therapy, therapeutic exercise, behavioral coaching and oral tadalafil. Median Erection Hardness Scale rose from 2 to 4 and median penile satisfaction from 2 to 5, both p < 0.01. All 32 reached an EHS of 3 or above by completion, against 8 of 32 (25%) at baseline. PROMIS Sexual Interest and Physical and Mental Component scores all improved, p < 0.01. Reported comorbidities included low back pain (53%), hip or groin pain (38%), pelvic floor pain (31%) and urinary symptoms (28%). The authors read the results as supporting an integrative model in which musculoskeletal and end-organ pathology initiates the syndrome and the nervous system amplifies it.
    By some distance the largest treated cohort described anywhere, and the reason to read it. It is also a preprint, so it has not been peer-reviewed, and it is retrospective and uncontrolled with unblinded patient-reported outcomes collected at the clinic delivering the treatment. Nine interventions were given simultaneously, so no effect can be attributed to any one of them. The most consequential limitation is in the endpoints: the Erection Hardness Scale measures erections, and none of the instruments used measures flaccid rigidity. The series demonstrates improvement in the symptoms that accompany HFS. Whether the defining sign changed is not a question these data can answer. The comorbidity figures are the most useful part, and the second-best of their kind after the 143-respondent survey.
    Comment on: Hard-flaccid syndrome: a report of two cases
    Colonnello E, Jannini EA. Int J Impot Res. 2026;38:144–145. doi:10.1038/s41443-025-01093-8 Correspondence
    Endorses the two-case report, highlighting that distinct traumatic triggers produced the same presentation and that both patients experienced significant distress despite normal laboratory and imaging results. Commends the use of validated assessment instruments and psychotropic medication, and proposes a penis–brain axis framing capturing how psychological and neurobiological factors interact.
    Short, but it marks the penis–brain axis idea moving from a suggestion inside a systematic review to a framing endorsed by an independent group. Whether that framing is productive or merely a label for what is not understood remains open.
    Persistent genital arousal disorder / genito-pelvic dysesthesia in all genders: ICSM 2024 recommendations
    Goldstein I, Komisaruk BR, Goldstein SW, Pukall C, Bilal A, Mueller J, Yee A, Bitzer J. Sex Med Rev. 2026;14(1):qeaf082. doi:10.1093/sxmrev/qeaf082 Expert consensus
    Extends PGAD/GPD to all genders, describing it as a sensory hyperactivity condition involving a broad range of undesired unrelenting dysesthesias, and names hard flaccid syndrome among them alongside genital arousal and sleep-related prolonged erection. Proposes a diagnostic algorithm examining five anatomical regions — end organ, pelvis/perineum, cauda equina, spinal cord, brain — with concurrent psychological and medical intervention.
    The current formal position on where HFS belongs. It gives a condition with no diagnostic criteria of its own an assessment framework borrowed from a related problem, which is a practical gain. It also creates an unresolved tension: PGAD/GPD is framed around sensory hyperactivity, while the dominant sensory complaint in HFS is loss, reported by 74.8% of surveyed patients. The consensus documents do not address that directly.

    Key points

    • The systematic review is the field’s reference point and reports eight studies, no trials, no criteria, 20% resolution.
    • Expert consensus now places HFS within the PGAD/GPD spectrum.
    • The classification imports a usable framework but leaves a hyperactivity-versus-loss tension unaddressed.
    • Thermal imaging produced the first objective measurement proposed for the condition: a colder flaccid glans, on 12 patients, in a congress abstract.
    • The largest treated cohort yet described, 32 men, appeared as a preprint of an uncontrolled nine-intervention protocol.

    10. What the corpus shows as a whole

    Reading twenty-one items in sequence produces observations that no individual paper makes.

    The evidence base is smaller than its influence. Adding every clinically assessed patient described anywhere gives roughly 162 individuals: 88 in the hemodynamic abstract, 32 in the rehabilitation preprint, 21 in the imaging abstract, 12 in the thermography abstract, 4 in the original series, and five more across the individual case reports. Some overlap between cohorts is possible and cannot be ruled out. A further 143 self-identified survey respondents were never clinically verified. That is the entire empirical foundation.

    The largest cohorts appear in the slightest formats. The three biggest — 88, 32 and 21 patients — are two conference abstracts and a preprint, none carrying the methods detail a full paper would require. The field’s most cited mechanistic proposal appears in a society newsletter. Meanwhile several full peer-reviewed papers describe one patient each. Format and information content are close to inversely related here.

    Almost nobody has used a control group. One study compares HFS patients against people without the condition, and its controls averaged thirteen years older — which matters for a measure as age-dependent as skin temperature. No other study here has any comparison group. That affects the annular tear finding most obviously, but it applies to every symptom frequency, comorbidity and imaging observation reported.

    Publication is concentrated. The majority of this literature appears in a single journal, and a small number of research groups account for most of it. That is normal for a new condition and it is not a criticism, but it means the field’s consensus rests on fewer independent judgments than the number of papers suggests.

    The picture has been stable since 2019. The symptom cluster described in the first four cases is substantially the cluster described in 2026. Seven years have added quantification, a mechanistic proposal, and a classification. They have not changed the clinical description, and they have not produced a diagnostic test or a treatment shown to work.

    Key points

    • Roughly 118 clinically assessed patients exist across the entire literature, with possible overlap.
    • The two largest cohorts are conference abstracts; the most influential mechanistic paper is a newsletter commentary.
    • No study has used a control group.
    • The clinical description has not materially changed since 2019.

    11. What is not in this list

    A literature index is also a record of absences, and these are the ones that matter most.

    • No randomized controlled trial of any intervention.1
    • No study with a matched control group, for any measure.
    • No direct measurement of sympathetic outflow to the penis — the central claim of the leading hypothesis.
    • No neurophysiological study: no quantitative sensory testing, no dorsal nerve conduction study, no electrodiagnostics of any kind.
    • No histological or imaging demonstration of the proposed injury to the dorsal nerve.
    • No prospective cohort recruited at presentation rather than from support communities.
    • No study reporting outcomes stratified by symptom duration.
    • No population-based prevalence estimate.2
    • No validated diagnostic criteria or symptom instrument specific to HFS.1

    Several of these would be inexpensive. Quantitative sensory testing against matched controls requires existing equipment and no new technique. Stratifying existing data by duration requires no new patients at all. The gap between what the field knows and what it could know without much difficulty is the most striking thing about this index.
