Mechanism · Article 07

Fourteen conjectures on the mechanism

Original hypotheses on what sustains the condition now, not on what started it.

66 min read · 29 references

Contents

    Read this first. This is not a summary of established knowledge. It is an exercise in hypothesis generation. Nothing proposed here has been demonstrated in a patient with hard flaccid syndrome, most of these conjectures will turn out to be wrong, and none of it should influence treatment decisions. Its only value is that each conjecture is stated precisely enough to be refuted by an experiment that could actually be performed.

    Summary

    • Fourteen mechanistic conjectures are proposed and sorted into three classes: neural, effector-autonomous, and structural.
    • The most informative available data are not the symptom surveys but the pattern of treatment failure. One observation dominates: sympathetic blockade at the superior hypogastric plexus — the anatomical funnel for the proposed mechanism — reportedly fails.
    • If that is accurate, the hard flaccid state cannot be a purely neurogenic phenomenon at the time it is treated. Something in the effector tissue has become autonomously hypercontractile.
    • Four candidate mechanisms could produce that autonomy: Rho-kinase-mediated calcium sensitization, denervation supersensitivity, co-transmitter dominance, and pacemaker dysregulation in interstitial cells.
    • Ketamine responses point centrally while block failures point peripherally. A two-compartment model reconciles them and predicts that every treatment’s efficacy depends on illness duration.
    Established published and replicated Hypothesis proposed, partially supported Speculative original conjecture, untested

    1. Declared assumptions

    Hypothesis generation has rules, and the first is that premises must be stated, because a conjecture inherits every weakness of what it assumes. This article rests on four assumptions. If any is wrong, large parts of what follows fail with it.

    One. The hard flaccid state is sustained contraction of cavernosal smooth muscle. It is the defining sign, reported by 90.9% of 143 surveyed patients,6 and normal flaccidity is itself maintained by tonic contraction of that tissue,11,12 so a pathologically firm flaccid penis is most economically read as an excess of the same process. Two. The initiating lesion is a peripheral nerve injury — in most cases a stretch injury sustained during vigorous masturbation or penile elongation practice, less commonly blunt perineal trauma to the pudendal nerve or sacral radiculopathy.1,4,5,9 Three. Worsening on standing reflects the physiological increase in sympathetic outflow that accompanies upright posture. Smooth muscle sympathetic nerve activity rises progressively with orthostatic stress through baroreflex resetting,20 so a symptom driven by sympathetic activity should worsen on standing, and 64.5% of patients report exactly that.6 Four. Pelvic floor hypertonicity, constipation and urinary symptoms are downstream consequences of the inciting injury and the resulting autonomic dysregulation, not causes of it.

    The fourth assumption is worth one sentence of defense. Cavernosal smooth muscle is involuntary tissue under autonomic control; the pelvic floor is striated tissue under somatic control.27 A striated muscle cannot set the resting tone of a smooth muscle it does not innervate. Where psychological factors matter is at the point of exposure — compulsive masturbation and body-image concerns are plausible routes to sustaining a mechanical injury — and not as a cause of the smooth muscle physiology that follows.

    2.3 The observation that reorganizes the problem

    Symptom frequencies describe what a condition looks like, and many mechanisms can produce the same appearance. Treatment responses describe where in a causal chain an intervention succeeds or fails, which is information about mechanism. That makes Table 2 the more valuable of the two despite being the weaker evidence, and one line in it does more work than the rest combined.

    The prevailing theory holds that excess sympathetic tone travels the hypogastric nerve to the erectile tissue.1 The superior hypogastric plexus is the anatomical funnel through which that traffic passes.25 A block placed there is therefore the direct test of the theory. It reportedly fails.

    If interrupting the nerve that is supposed to be carrying the signal does not abolish the sign, then by the time the patient is treated the sign is no longer being carried by that nerve. Either the route is different, or the effector no longer needs the route.

    That fork organizes this entire article. Conjectures I to IV pursue the first branch: the neural signal is real but travels differently, or arises somewhere other than assumed. Conjectures V to VIII pursue the second and more interesting branch: the cavernosal smooth muscle has become autonomously hypercontractile and no longer requires ongoing neural drive. Conjectures IX to XI concern tissue that has already been remodeled. Conjectures XII to XIV address the sensory and vascular findings.

    A caution before proceeding. The entire fork rests on unverified reports of block failure in which block adequacy was never confirmed.

    Key points

    • Treatment-failure patterns constrain mechanism more tightly than symptom lists do.
    • Reported failure of superior hypogastric plexus block is the single most consequential observation available.
    • It forces a choice: either the efferent route differs from the assumed one, or the effector tissue has become independent of it.
    • The observation is unverified and no reported block was confirmed adequate.

    3. Class A — Conjectures locating the lesion in the nervous system

    Conjecture I · The sacral splanchnic bypass Speculative

    Claim. Pathological sympathetic outflow reaches the erectile tissue substantially through the sacral splanchnic nerves arising from the sacral sympathetic trunk, rather than exclusively through the hypogastric nerves descending from the superior hypogastric plexus.

    Mechanism. The inferior hypogastric plexus — the final relay to the pelvic organs — receives sympathetic input by two anatomically separate routes: the hypogastric nerves from the superior hypogastric plexus, and the sacral splanchnic nerves from the sacral portion of the sympathetic trunk.25 A superior hypogastric plexus block interrupts the first only. A block on the paravertebral chain at lumbar levels interrupts fibers descending within the chain toward the sacral ganglia, and so affects the second.

    Motivation. The reported dissociation: superior hypogastric plexus blocks mostly fail, lumbar chain blocks at L2–L4 have occasionally resolved the state. Under the standard account this is backwards.

    What is established and what is not. The dual route is established anatomy.25 That the minor route carries the pathological traffic is entirely speculative — and the same source describes the superior hypogastric plexus as the larger contributor, which counts against the conjecture. For it to work, the smaller route must be sufficient alone. That is possible if the tone–drive relationship is saturating rather than linear, but that is a second conjecture stacked on the first.

    What it explains. The block dissociation, and the single reported case in which an L2 block reduced scrotal retraction while leaving the hard flaccid state unchanged. Scrotal position is governed by dartos smooth muscle whose efferent supply differs in level and route from that of the cavernosal tissue; a theory that predicts these outputs can be dissociated is supported by an observation in which they were. The failure of L1 blocks is not explained and may be technical.

    Tests. Systematic comparison of superior hypogastric plexus, lumbar chain at several levels, and ganglion impar blocks, with two requirements the existing reports lack: objective confirmation of block adequacy by skin temperature rise or sympathogalvanic response, and an objective measure of penile resting tone rather than patient impression.

    Full write-up. A standalone PDF of this conjecture is available: Conjecture I — The sacral splanchnic bypass (PDF).

    Assessment

    • Explains the block dissociation and the scrotal–cavernosal dissociation.
    • Requires the anatomically minor route to be functionally sufficient.
    • Does not explain L1 failure.
    • Plausibility: Moderate.

    Conjecture II · NMDA-dependent central maintenance Speculative

    Claim. The peripheral injury initiates but does not maintain. What maintains is a spinal process — an NMDA receptor-dependent increase in excitability within circuits driving sympathetic preganglionic neurons — that becomes independent of the original afferent source.

    Mechanism. A stretch injury generates an intense, prolonged afferent barrage. Nociceptor input of that kind triggers a prolonged increase in the excitability and synaptic efficacy of neurons in central nociceptive pathways, and both wind-up and central sensitization depend on glutamate acting at NMDA receptors.21 Somatic afferent input is separately known to produce reflex changes in sympathetic efferent activity and thereby in effector organ function, across many organ systems.22 The conjecture is that these two established phenomena meet: sensitization occurs in the somato-autonomic limb, not only the nociceptive one, leaving sympathetic preganglionic output tonically elevated.

    Motivation. Pudendal blocks and pudendal decompression both fail — expected if the peripheral generator has become irrelevant. Ketamine, a non-competitive NMDA antagonist, has reportedly resolved or improved the state. And the condition persists for years after an injury that should have healed.

    What it does not explain. The 42% reporting no inciting event.6 It can absorb them by proposing subclinical injury, but absorbing is not predicting. Nor does it explain why remission is so rare when central sensitization is described as reversible in principle.21 It also cannot, by itself, explain the failure of sympathetic blockade: if elevated spinal drive is the sole mechanism, blocking the outflow downstream should still work. This is the conjecture’s most serious problem and it is the reason Class B exists.

    A caveat about ketamine specifically. Ketamine has pronounced psychoactive effects. A drug that alters perception is a difficult drug to evaluate by self-report of a symptom the patient assesses by touch and appearance. The reported responses may be real; they may also be artifacts of an uncontrolled setting.

    Tests. Quantitative sensory testing against matched controls, looking for the signatures of central sensitization: enhanced temporal summation, secondary hyperalgesia, aftersensations.21 Non-invasive, existing equipment, no new technology. Then a controlled ketamine infusion study with an objective tone endpoint and saline control.

    Full write-up. A standalone PDF of this conjecture is available: Conjecture II — NMDA-dependent central maintenance (PDF).

    Assessment

    • Explains chronicity, failure of every peripherally directed intervention, and the ketamine reports.
    • Does not explain sympathetic block failure, which is a serious problem.
    • Testable now by quantitative sensory testing.
    • Plausibility: Moderate to High.

    Conjecture III · Sympathetic–sensory coupling in the dorsal root ganglion Speculative

    Theory originally coined by HFS community member Literature Greedy

    Claim. After the injury, sympathetic fibers sprout into the dorsal root ganglia serving the penis and form functional contacts with sensory neurons, creating a coupling in which sympathetic activity directly drives sensory discharge — and, through spinal reflex, further sympathetic activity.

    Mechanism. This is not invented for HFS. After sciatic nerve ligation in the rat, noradrenergic perivascular axons sprout into the dorsal root ganglion and form basket-like structures around large-diameter axotomized sensory neurons, and sympathetic stimulation can then activate those neurons repetitively.23 Applied here: the stretch injury to the dorsal nerve triggers such sprouting in the corresponding ganglia. Sympathetic outflow — which rises on standing, with stress, and with sexual stimulation — now directly excites sensory neurons.20,23 Those sensory volleys feed the somato-autonomic reflex described in Conjecture II, which raises sympathetic outflow further. The loop closes inside the ganglion.

    Why this is attractive. It explains something no other conjecture does: why a condition whose cardinal sign is a smooth muscle phenomenon should be so tightly coupled to sensory symptoms, and why sympathetic block might relieve sensory complaints in patients whose hard flaccid state does not change. It also explains why symptoms worsen with masturbation in 75.9% of patients6 — sexual arousal and orgasm involve substantial sympathetic activation, which under this model directly drives the sensitized sensory apparatus.

    Established versus speculative. Established: sympathetic sprouting into the DRG after peripheral nerve injury, and functional sympathetic-sensory coupling.23 Speculative: that it occurs in the ganglia serving the penis in HFS; that it is functionally significant here; and the entire loop closure.

    The awkward datum. Dorsal root ganglion stimulation at L2 reportedly failed in one patient. If the ganglion were the critical site, neuromodulation there might be expected to help. One patient at one level is not decisive, and the relevant ganglia for penile innervation are sacral rather than L2, so this may be a test of the wrong level rather than of the conjecture.

    Tests. Whether a sympathetic block relieves dysesthesia and pain even when it does not change the hard flaccid state — a dissociation this conjecture predicts and others do not. In tissue: examination of sacral DRG for perivascular noradrenergic basket formations, which requires post-mortem or surgical material and will be difficult to obtain.

    Full write-up. A standalone PDF of this conjecture is available: Conjecture III — Sympathetic–sensory coupling in the dorsal root ganglion (PDF).

    Assessment

    • Built on a well-replicated phenomenon in nerve injury models.
    • Uniquely explains tight sympathetic–sensory coupling and worsening with masturbation.
    • Predicts that sympathetic blockade should relieve sensory symptoms independently of the flaccid state.
    • Plausibility: Moderate.

    Conjecture IV · Ectopic afferent generator from channel remodeling Speculative

    Claim. The injured dorsal nerve axons and their neighbors remodel their voltage-gated sodium channel expression, producing a persistent ectopic discharge generator that continues firing regardless of local conditions at the injury site.

    Mechanism. Upregulation of particular sodium channel subtypes contributes to ectopic discharge in sensory neurons after nerve injury, and the pattern is phase-dependent: in the acute phase Nav1.8 is downregulated in injured neurons and upregulated in neighboring uninjured neurons, contributing to spontaneous discharge, while in the chronic phase Nav1.8 is upregulated in injured neurons too, further increasing and maintaining ectopic discharge.24 That is a mechanism for an afferent barrage that outlives its cause.

    Why it matters here. It supplies the missing ingredient for Conjecture II. Central sensitization is described as reversible in principle;21 a condition that almost never remits needs something continuing to feed it. A remodeled ectopic generator would do that. It also explains the failure of decompression surgery in a way that is more specific than “the problem is central”: if the generator is distributed along the axon and in the ganglion rather than located at a compression point, then releasing the compression removes nothing.

    Accounting for gabapentin. Gabapentin reportedly helps pain but not the hard flaccid state. It acts on the α2δ subunit of voltage-gated calcium channels to reduce excitatory transmission from sensitized afferents. Under this conjecture that is exactly what would be expected: it dampens the consequences of the ectopic barrage in the pain pathway without abolishing the generator or the sympathetic efferent limb.

    Tests. Quantitative sensory testing showing stimulus-independent pain and paresthesia in a dorsal nerve distribution. A diagnostic trial of a sodium channel blocking agent under controlled conditions, with the prediction that it should reduce paresthesia and dysesthesia while leaving the flaccid state unchanged.

    Full write-up. A standalone PDF of this conjecture is available: Conjecture IV — Ectopic afferent generator from channel remodeling (PDF).

    Assessment

    • Provides a persistent afferent driver, which Conjecture II requires but does not supply.
    • Explains why decompression fails and why gabapentin helps only pain.
    • Does not by itself explain the smooth muscle state.
    • Plausibility: Moderate.

    4. Class B — Conjectures locating the lesion in the effector tissue

    This class exists because of one line in Table 2. If sympathetic blockade at the appropriate level genuinely fails to abolish the hard flaccid state, then at the time of treatment the contraction is not being sustained by ongoing neural traffic. It is being sustained by the smooth muscle itself. Four mechanisms could produce that, and they are not mutually exclusive.

    Conjecture V · Rho-kinase-mediated calcium sensitization Speculative

    Claim. Sustained sympathetic stimulation upregulates RhoA/Rho-kinase signaling in cavernosal smooth muscle. The tissue becomes contracted at calcium concentrations that would normally leave it relaxed, and the contraction therefore persists when the neural signal is removed.

    Why this is the most important conjecture in the article. Smooth muscle contraction is not governed by calcium alone. RhoA and its effector Rho-kinase increase myosin light chain phosphorylation by inhibiting myosin light chain phosphatase, which raises the tissue’s sensitivity to whatever calcium is present.16 In the penis this is not a marginal pathway: RhoA-mediated calcium sensitization is one of the mechanisms maintaining the normal flaccid state, high RhoA expression contributes to that state, and it can be reversed by a Rho-kinase inhibitor.15 Elevated RhoA/Rho-kinase activity is an established mechanism of erectile dysfunction in disease states.16

    A tissue whose calcium sensitivity has been turned up is contracted without needing an ongoing signal. That is precisely the property the block failures demand.

    It also resolves the phentolamine dispute. α-blockade reduces adrenergically driven calcium entry.18 It does not reverse calcium sensitization, which operates downstream at the level of myosin phosphatase. So under this conjecture, α-blockade would fail to normalize resting tone — while a sufficiently large pharmacological push through the intact nitric oxide pathway could still produce erection, because erection recruits a different and more powerful relaxation mechanism. That is exactly the disputed observation: the penis becomes erect but does not become normally soft. The conjecture predicts the dispute.

    It predicts a specific discriminating experiment. If calcium sensitization maintains the state, a Rho-kinase inhibitor should normalize resting tone, and should do so at doses that do not by themselves produce erection. No other conjecture in this article predicts that. Rho-kinase inhibitors exist and have been studied in human isolated corpora cavernosa. This is stated as a research proposal only and is emphatically not a treatment recommendation.

    Established versus speculative. Established: the pathway, its role in maintaining normal flaccidity, its reversibility by Rho-kinase inhibition, and its upregulation in disease.15,16 Speculative: that it is upregulated in HFS. Nobody has measured RhoA or Rho-kinase activity in tissue from an HFS patient, and obtaining such tissue is the obvious practical obstacle.

    Tests. The dose-controlled Rho-kinase inhibitor challenge described above. In tissue, if any becomes available: RhoA and Rho-kinase expression and activity against controls.

    Full write-up. A standalone PDF of this conjecture is available: Conjecture V — Rho-kinase-mediated calcium sensitization (PDF).

    Assessment

    • Supplies effector autonomy, which the block failures demand and no neural conjecture provides.
    • Predicts that α-blockade produces erection without normalizing resting tone — the disputed phentolamine observation.
    • Generates a unique discriminating experiment.
    • Plausibility: Moderate to High. The highest of the fourteen.

    Conjecture VI · Denervation supersensitivity Speculative

    Claim. The stretch injury partially denervates the cavernosal tissue. The denervated smooth muscle becomes supersensitive, so that normal or even reduced catecholamine exposure produces exaggerated contraction.

    Mechanism. Denervation supersensitivity is a general property of effector tissue. After destruction of a postganglionic sympathetic neuron, postsynaptic receptors upregulate and supersensitivity to exogenous transmitter develops within days to weeks. It has two components: a presynaptic one from loss of the terminal and its reuptake mechanism, which increases the concentration of transmitter reaching the receptor, and a slower postsynaptic one from increased receptor number or signaling efficiency. In smooth muscle specifically, the supersensitivity that develops is described as nonspecific rather than confined to one receptor type.

    The elegant part. A supersensitive effector explains block failure without requiring the neural signal to be abnormal at all. Sympathetic blockade interrupts nerve traffic; it does not remove circulating catecholamines, and a supersensitive tissue with an impaired reuptake mechanism will respond to those. It also explains orthostatic worsening in a second way: standing raises sympathetic outflow and circulating catecholamines,20 and a supersensitive tissue amplifies that ordinary physiological rise into a symptom.

    And it explains the variable α-blocker response. If supersensitivity is nonspecific rather than receptor-specific, α-blockade will only partially reverse it. If it is receptor-specific in some patients, α-blockade will work well in those patients. Variable response across patients is what a mechanism of variable character would produce.

    Weakness. Classical denervation supersensitivity follows destruction of the postganglionic neuron. A stretch injury to the dorsal nerve — a somatic sensory branch — is not obviously a sympathetic denervating lesion. For this conjecture to work, the injury must also damage autonomic fibers, which a violent stretch of the whole organ plausibly could, but which nobody has demonstrated.

    Tests. Dose-response comparison of intracavernosal α-agonist between HFS patients and controls: supersensitivity predicts a leftward shift. This is a clean experiment, though it requires exposing patients to a contractile agent and would need careful ethical justification.

    Full write-up. A standalone PDF of this conjecture is available: Conjecture VI — Denervation supersensitivity (PDF).

    Assessment

    • Explains block failure without requiring abnormal neural traffic.
    • Explains orthostatic worsening and variable α-blocker response.
    • Requires autonomic denervation that has not been demonstrated.
    • Plausibility: Moderate.

    Conjecture VII · Co-transmitter dominance Speculative

    Claim. Under prolonged high-frequency sympathetic firing, the contractile signal shifts away from noradrenaline toward co-transmitters — principally neuropeptide Y — producing a contraction that α-blockade cannot fully reverse.

    Mechanism. Sympathetic nerves do not release noradrenaline alone. Neuropeptide Y is a sympathetic co-transmitter present in human penile tissue, where it modulates noradrenergic vasoconstriction in penile small arteries through Y1 and Y2 postsynaptic receptors and an atypical endothelial receptor.13 Constriction of this kind is characteristically resistant to α-adrenoceptor blockade. Sustained high-frequency firing favors co-transmitter release, so the pharmacological character of the contraction should drift over time.

    The prediction that makes it worth testing. α-blocker responsiveness should decline with duration of illness. That is falsifiable, and it is checkable retrospectively from existing case records without any new experiment. If early patients respond and late patients do not, the conjecture gains real support. If response is randomly distributed with respect to duration, it is in trouble.

    Relation to Conjecture V. These two are competitors for the same explanatory job — accounting for incomplete α-blocker response — and they are distinguishable. Co-transmitter dominance predicts that a Y1 receptor antagonist adds benefit to α-blockade; calcium sensitization predicts that a Rho-kinase inhibitor does the job alone. Both could be operating.

    Tests. The duration correlation above. A Y1 antagonist challenge. Measurement of neuropeptide Y in cavernosal blood aspirated during a clinically indicated procedure, against systemic levels and controls.

    Full write-up. A standalone PDF of this conjecture is available: Conjecture VII — Co-transmitter dominance (PDF).

    Assessment

    • Grounded in established vascular pharmacology and a co-transmitter documented in penile tissue.
    • Explains variable α-blocker response and phentolamine ambiguity.
    • Yields a prediction checkable from existing records.
    • Plausibility: Moderate.

    Conjecture VIII · Pacemaker dysregulation Speculative

    Claim. Interstitial pacemaker cells within the corpus cavernosum, which generate spontaneous rhythmic activity that sets baseline tone, become chronically upregulated after sustained noradrenergic exposure — producing autonomous contractile activity that continues without neural input.

    Mechanism. Interstitial cells resembling the interstitial cells of Cajal have been described in the lower urinary tract and penis, where they interact with smooth muscle and contribute to spontaneous activity.17 The pacemaker mechanism depends on calcium release from ryanodine- and inositol trisphosphate-sensitive stores, and — this is the critical detail — it is inhibited by nitric oxide acting through cyclic GMP and enhanced by noradrenaline.17

    Why that detail matters. If noradrenaline enhances the pacemaker, then a period of sustained sympathetic overactivity does not merely contract the smooth muscle while it lasts. It drives up an oscillator that then keeps running. The tissue acquires its own rhythm. Removing the neural input at that point would be like unplugging a metronome that has already been wound.

    This is the most direct mechanism in the article for genuine effector autonomy, and it makes a further prediction that distinguishes it from all others: the hard flaccid state should not be perfectly steady. It should fluctuate on a timescale set by the oscillator rather than by neural drive. Patients do describe variability that does not track obvious triggers. That is weak evidence, but it is evidence of the right kind.

    Established versus speculative. Established: interstitial cells in penile tissue, their pacemaker activity, and its modulation by nitric oxide and noradrenaline.17 Speculative: essentially everything about their role in HFS. This conjecture has the widest gap between its mechanistic elegance and its evidential support.

    Tests. High-resolution measurement of resting penile tone over hours, looking for oscillatory structure. Its presence would support the conjecture; a perfectly flat elevated tone would weaken it.

    Full write-up. A standalone PDF of this conjecture is available: Conjecture VIII — Pacemaker dysregulation (PDF).

    Assessment

    • Provides the most direct route to true effector autonomy.
    • Rests on the documented finding that noradrenaline enhances the penile pacemaker.
    • Uniquely predicts oscillatory rather than steady elevated tone.
    • Plausibility: Low to Moderate. Elegant but almost wholly untested.

    5. Class C — Conjectures about tissue that has already changed

    Conjecture IX · Segmental tone heterogeneity in a coupled syncytium Speculative

    Claim. Hourglassing, bottlenecking and lateral tilt are produced by regional differences in smooth muscle tone within a gap-junction-coupled syncytium. They are not tunical lesions, and they resolve at full erection because maximal pressure and maximal relaxation override the regional difference.

    The deduction. This conjecture is forced by an observation rather than chosen, and the reasoning can be checked at each step. In Peyronie’s disease a fibrous tunical plaque produces indentation or curvature that worsens with erection, because inelastic tissue cannot expand while the surrounding tunica does.28 The hourglassing reported in HFS does the opposite: present when flaccid or partially erect, gone at full erection. Whatever produces it must therefore be something maximal intracavernosal pressure can overcome — and a tunical lesion is by definition not overcome by pressure. So the deformity is not tunical, and it is pressure-dependent.

    A pressure-dependent segmental narrowing is what a band of tissue holding more tone than its neighbors would produce. At low pressure the band constrains the contour; at full erection, nitric oxide-driven relaxation and high pressure equalize the geometry and the waist disappears.

    The substrate exists. Corporal smooth muscle is sparsely innervated, and its cells are coupled by gap junctions formed of connexin43, which provide the mechanism of intercellular communication coordinating contraction and relaxation across the tissue.14 A syncytium that behaves as coupled regional units is one in which uneven input produces a coherent band rather than scattered individual cell contraction.

    What else it explains. Lateral tilt as asymmetric tone between the paired corpora — an asymmetric deformity in a paired structure with segmental innervation is what this predicts. And it explains why patients insist their deformity behaves unlike Peyronie’s: it does, because it is a tone phenomenon rather than a structural one.

    Tests. Dynamic ultrasound of the flaccid and progressively erect penis with the narrowing site mapped at graded pressures. The conjecture predicts the waist is reproducible within a patient, that its cross-sectional area increases with pressure while the tunica remains sonographically normal, and that it corresponds to a nerve distribution territory or a remembered injury site. Peyronie’s predicts a visible tunical abnormality and worsening with pressure. The two are cleanly distinguishable by an examination that is already routine.

    Full write-up. A standalone PDF of this conjecture is available: Conjecture IX — Segmental tone heterogeneity in a coupled syncytium (PDF).

    Assessment

    • Forced by the pressure-dependence of the deformity; a tunical lesion is excluded on logical grounds.
    • Connexin43 coupling supplies the substrate for regional behavior.
    • Also accounts for lateral tilt.
    • Plausibility: Moderate. The cleanest deduction here, and among the easiest to test.

    Conjecture X · The hypoxia–fibrosis ratchet Speculative

    Claim. Loss of nocturnal erections removes the tissue’s principal reoxygenation mechanism. Chronic cavernosal hypoxia drives fibrotic remodeling, which reduces compliance, which further impairs erection. The condition converts itself from a functional disorder into a structural one, largely in one direction.

    Mechanism. Cavernosal oxygen tension is low in flaccidity and rises during erection; nocturnal erections are how the tissue is regularly reoxygenated. In the rat, cavernous neurotomy produces hypoxia in the corpus cavernosum together with significantly increased protein and messenger RNA expression of transforming growth factor β1, a cytokine that induces fibrosis, with resulting collagenization.19 In HFS the chain would run: sustained contraction reduces perfusion; loss of nocturnal erections removes reoxygenation; hypoxia raises TGF-β1; collagen accumulates; compliance falls; veno-occlusive function degrades; erections worsen; nocturnal erections fall further. Each turn makes the next easier and the reverse harder.

    Motivation. Loss of morning and nocturnal erections is documented across the case literature.7,9 Symptom resolution occurs in only 20% of patients across pooled studies.5 And mild corporal fibrosis was found on Doppler in a published case — one patient, but a finding a purely functional model does not anticipate.10

    The inference worth attention. This conjecture explains the mixed PDE5 inhibitor response without requiring patients to differ constitutionally. If early HFS is tone-dominant and late HFS is structure-dominant, a drug promoting smooth muscle relaxation should work better early and progressively less well as collagen replaces smooth muscle. Mixed response becomes a function of disease stage rather than of patient type — an apparently random clinical observation reframed as a predicted gradient.

    Tests. Ultrasound elastography or magnetic resonance imaging of corporal tissue stratified by symptom duration against age-matched controls. Formal nocturnal penile tumescence monitoring. Correlation of PDE5 inhibitor response against duration in existing records.

    Full write-up. A standalone PDF of this conjecture is available: Conjecture X — The hypoxia–fibrosis ratchet (PDF).

    Assessment

    • Explains progression, the 20% resolution rate, and the reported corporal fibrosis.
    • Reframes mixed PDE5 inhibitor response as a stage effect.
    • Extrapolated from animal neurotomy models rather than observed in HFS.
    • Plausibility: Moderate to High.

    Conjecture XI · Endothelial dysfunction and impaired relaxation reserve Speculative

    Claim. Chronic hypoxia and contraction impair endothelial nitric oxide production in the cavernosal sinusoids. The problem becomes not only excess contraction but deficient relaxation capacity.

    Mechanism. Erection depends on nitric oxide raising cyclic guanosine monophosphate in smooth muscle, causing relaxation, with sinusoidal expansion then compressing subtunical venules to trap blood.11,12 Cavernosal endothelium is a major source of that nitric oxide. Sustained contraction with reduced perfusion is an unfavorable environment for endothelial function, and phosphodiesterase type 5 inhibitors act by prolonging a cyclic GMP signal that must first be generated.12

    What it explains that Conjecture X does not. Why PDE5 inhibitors fail in some patients who do not yet have fibrosis. A drug that amplifies an existing signal cannot work well if the signal is not being produced. This predicts a specific pattern: patients failing PDE5 inhibitors should include a group who respond to agents acting downstream of nitric oxide production — which is the pharmacological logic behind intracavernosal prostaglandin agents, though this article makes no treatment recommendation.

    It also offers a second explanation for glans insufficiency: the glans depends on spongiosal filling driven by flow, and an endothelium that cannot deliver relaxation signals will fail there before it fails in the tunica-enclosed corpora.

    Weakness. This is the least specific conjecture in the article. Endothelial dysfunction is invoked in many conditions and explains a great deal loosely without predicting much sharply. Its presence in HFS is entirely inferred.

    Tests. Assessment of endothelium-dependent versus endothelium-independent relaxation in HFS patients, comparing response to agents that require endothelial nitric oxide against those that do not.

    Full write-up. A standalone PDF of this conjecture is available: Conjecture XI — Endothelial dysfunction and impaired relaxation reserve (PDF).

    Assessment

    • Reframes the problem as deficient relaxation as well as excess contraction.
    • Explains PDE5 inhibitor failure in patients without fibrosis.
    • Low specificity; explains loosely rather than predicting sharply.
    • Plausibility: Low to Moderate.

    6. Class D — Conjectures about sensation, inflammation and outflow

    Conjecture XII · Partial deafferentation with central remapping Speculative

    Claim. The sensory picture — numbness coexisting with dysesthesia and paresthesia, and the report that the penis feels hollow, disconnected or not part of the body — is the signature of partial deafferentation followed by central hyperexcitability and degraded cortical representation.

    Mechanism. A stretch injury to the dorsal nerve of the penis, the terminal sensory branch of the pudendal nerve,26 produces partial axonal loss. Fewer functioning afferents means reduced tactile transmission — numbness, loss of temperature, pressure, vibration and texture discrimination, reduced erogenous sensation. The partially deafferented central neurons undergo the NMDA-dependent increase in excitability described in Conjecture II,21 generating spontaneous and evoked abnormal sensation in the same territory. A cortical representation deprived of reliable input then degrades, and the organ’s place in the body schema becomes unstable.

    Why it matters beyond symptom accounting. It resolves a contradiction in the current classification. Expert consensus places HFS within persistent genital arousal disorder / genito-pelvic dysesthesia, a spectrum characterized as sensory hyperactivity.3 Yet the dominant sensory complaint is sensory loss, reported by 74.8%.6 Under partial deafferentation these are not merely compatible but expected: reduced input and increased central gain are two consequences of one lesion.

    The weakest element. The body-ownership symptom. Altered ownership of a body part is a real phenomenon in neurology, but no study has examined it in HFS, no mechanism has been demonstrated, and the inference from deafferentation to disrupted body schema is several steps long. This is the most speculative claim in the article. It is included because the symptom is reported consistently and nothing else accounts for it at all.

    Tests. Quantitative sensory testing with modality-by-modality mapping, and comparison of the numb territory against the known distribution of the dorsal nerve.26 If the deficit maps onto that distribution, a peripheral lesion is supported; if it is glove-like or crosses distributions, a central explanation gains ground.

    Full write-up. A standalone PDF of this conjecture is available: Conjecture XII — Partial deafferentation with central remapping (PDF).

    Assessment

    • Explains simultaneous sensory loss and abnormal sensation.
    • Resolves the tension between HFS being hypoesthetic and PGAD/GPD being a hyperactivity spectrum.2
    • The body-ownership element is the least supported claim in this article.
    • Plausibility: Moderate for the sensory component; Low for cortical remapping.

    Conjecture XIII · Neuroinflammatory maintenance Speculative

    Claim. Spinal microglial activation following the injury sustains the altered dorsal horn state, converting what would otherwise be a transient sensitization into a persistent one.

    Mechanism. Peripheral nerve injury activates spinal microglia, which release mediators including brain-derived neurotrophic factor and proinflammatory cytokines, producing enduring changes in dorsal horn neurons that contribute to central sensitization and persistent pain.29 This is a maintenance mechanism rather than an initiating one, which is exactly the role HFS needs filled: the puzzle is not why symptoms start but why they never stop.

    What it adds. Conjecture II proposes central sensitization but cannot explain its permanence, since sensitization is described as reversible in principle.21 Conjecture IV supplies one answer — a continuing peripheral generator. This supplies another — a glial state that maintains itself. The two are not exclusive and may be sequential.

    The honest problem. Neuroinflammation is invoked so widely in chronic pain that its explanatory value is diluted. It predicts little that is specific to HFS. It is included because the maintenance problem is real and because it points at a distinct class of intervention, but it should be regarded as a placeholder for a mechanism rather than a mechanism.

    Tests. Difficult in humans. Positron emission tomography with translocator protein ligands has been used to image glial activation in other pain conditions and would be the obvious approach, though it is expensive and available in few centers.

    Full write-up. A standalone PDF of this conjecture is available: Conjecture XIII — Neuroinflammatory maintenance (PDF).

    Assessment

    • Addresses the maintenance problem that Conjecture II leaves open.
    • Grounded in well-replicated nerve injury biology.
    • Low specificity to HFS; hard to test in humans.
    • Plausibility: Low to Moderate.

    Conjecture XIV · Venous outflow contribution Speculative

    Claim. A subset of patients has a genuine venous outflow component that accounts for engorged and prominent veins, discoloration, and the distended fluid-filled quality of the “long flaccid” state.

    The direction problem. Two directions are possible and the available data cannot separate them: chronic sympathetic outflow alters pelvic venous tone and congestion follows; or pelvic venous congestion exists independently and contributes directly. Reported frequent positive findings for pelvic venous congestion are compatible with both — and with neither being causal, since such findings occur in asymptomatic people and prevalence in patients proves nothing without matched controls.

    Hard flaccid and long flaccid as two states of one system. The “long flaccid” description — a penis more extended than it should be, feeling either firm or like a balloon filled with water — is hard to reconcile with pure contraction, because contraction shortens. It reconciles easily with a congestive state: trapped venous blood distends the organ while trabecular tone is comparatively lower. This suggests the two are not different diseases but different balances within one system, one tone-dominant and one congestion-dominant, which would explain why individual patients report alternating between them.

    A note on orthostatic worsening. This article assumes, with the physiology, that worsening on standing reflects the increase in sympathetic outflow that accompanies upright posture.20 That assumption is reasonable and is adopted throughout. It is worth recording that gravitational venous pooling occurs simultaneously and would predict the same symptom, so the orthostatic observation is consistent with the sympathetic account without being uniquely diagnostic of it.

    Tests. Pelvic magnetic resonance venography in patients against matched asymptomatic controls with blinded reading. Penile Doppler supine and standing in the same patients. Comparison of venous findings between patients reporting predominantly hard flaccid and predominantly long flaccid presentations, which this conjecture predicts should differ.

    Full write-up. A standalone PDF of this conjecture is available: Conjecture XIV — Venous outflow contribution (PDF).

    Assessment

    • Accounts for engorged veins, discoloration and the distended “long flaccid” state.
    • Proposes hard flaccid and long flaccid as tone-dominant and congestion-dominant states of one system.
    • Causal direction undetermined; base rate problem is serious.
    • Plausibility: Low to Moderate.

    9. Common themes

    Three themes run through the set, and they are more informative than any single conjecture.

    Migration. Every conjecture that explains chronicity involves the lesion moving away from where it started. The injury is peripheral; the maintenance is proposed to be spinal, or ganglionic, or intrinsic to the smooth muscle, or structural. This is why interventions aimed at the original injury site fail, and it predicts that the correct target changes over time.

    Autonomy. Four separate and independently established mechanisms — calcium sensitization, denervation supersensitivity, co-transmitter dominance and pacemaker upregulation — can each convert a driven contraction into a self-sustaining one. That so many routes to autonomy exist in this tissue is itself notable, and it suggests effector autonomy should be the default expectation in a chronically overdriven smooth muscle rather than an exotic hypothesis.

    Ratchets. Several proposed loops are asymmetric: easy to enter, hard to leave. Hypoxia begets fibrosis begets hypoxia. Sensitization begets guarding begets afferent input begets sensitization. A condition built from ratchets will show exactly the epidemiology HFS shows — rare remission, poor treatment response, and outcomes that worsen with time.

    10. A unified model in two compartments

    The central tension in this article is between two facts that point in opposite directions. Ketamine reportedly works, which points centrally. Sympathetic blockade reportedly fails, which points peripherally. A single-compartment model cannot hold both.

    A two-compartment model can. Propose that HFS involves a central compartment — sensitized spinal circuitry generating elevated sympathetic outflow, maintained by ectopic afferent input and glial activation — and a peripheral compartment — cavernosal smooth muscle that has become autonomously hypercontractile through calcium sensitization, supersensitivity, co-transmitter dominance or pacemaker upregulation. The compartments are established sequentially: the central one first, the peripheral one after prolonged exposure to its output.

    Table 5 · The two-compartment staged model
    Stage Dominant compartment What should work What should fail
    1 · Acute (weeks) Peripheral injury; central not yet established Removal of the provoking activity; possibly early afferent blockade Nothing yet tested at this stage
    2 · Central (months) Sensitized spinal circuitry driving outflow Centrally acting agents; sympathetic blockade should still work here Interventions at the original injury site
    3 · Dual (months to years) Both; effector becoming autonomous Combination targeting both compartments Either alone — which may be why single interventions look so inconsistent
    4 · Effector-autonomous / structural (years) Peripheral, plus fibrosis Agents acting on the effector: calcium desensitization, co-transmitter antagonism Sympathetic blockade; centrally acting agents; anything requiring healthy tissue

    The model’s most useful property is that it makes the apparent incoherence of the treatment literature predictable. Mixed responses to α-blockers, PDE5 inhibitors, ketamine and nerve blocks would not be evidence that these agents are unreliable. They would be evidence that the disease is a moving target and that patients are being pooled across stages.

    This yields one immediate, cheap and high-value recommendation for anyone studying this condition: stratify by duration. Every study of HFS conducted so far has treated patients as a single population. If this model has any merit, that practice is destroying signal that already exists in collected data.

    Key points

    • Ketamine responses and block failures point in opposite directions and require two compartments.
    • The central compartment is proposed to establish first, the peripheral one after prolonged exposure.
    • The model predicts that treatment efficacy depends on illness stage.
    • Its immediate implication is that all analyses should be stratified by symptom duration.

    11. The highest-priority unanswered questions

    1. Is sympathetic outflow to the penis actually elevated in HFS? The central claim of every theory including all of these, never measured in a patient.
    2. Do HFS patients show quantitative signs of central sensitization? Answerable this year with existing equipment. The highest-value study available.
    3. Is RhoA/Rho-kinase activity elevated in HFS cavernosal tissue? Would establish or exclude the leading effector-autonomy mechanism.
    4. Does a Rho-kinase inhibitor normalize resting tone at sub-erectogenic doses? Would discriminate calcium sensitization from every neural conjecture in one experiment.
    5. Does phentolamine normalize resting tone or only produce erection? Unresolved since the prevailing theory was proposed and resolvable by dose-ranging.
    6. Were the reported block failures technically adequate blocks? Without confirmation, the entire dataset that motivates Class B is uninterpretable.
    7. Does α-blocker and PDE5 inhibitor response decline with duration? Checkable retrospectively from existing records.
    8. Is there measurable corporal fibrosis, and does it track duration? Distinguishes functional from structural disease.
    9. Does the sensory deficit map onto the dorsal nerve distribution? Distinguishes peripheral from central origin.
    10. Is resting tone steady or oscillatory? A single prolonged measurement would test the pacemaker conjecture.
    11. What distinguishes the 42% with no inciting event from the 58% with one? Possibly two conditions sharing a presentation. Possibly response bias.

    12. What would refute this framework

    A hypothesis that cannot fail is not worth proposing, so the conditions are stated. The framework would be substantially damaged by: direct measurement showing normal or reduced sympathetic outflow to the penis in symptomatic patients; quantitative sensory testing showing no central sensitization signature against matched controls; demonstration that a technically confirmed sympathetic block reliably abolishes the hard flaccid state, which would collapse Class B entirely and return the mechanism to a straightforwardly neurogenic account; normal RhoA/Rho-kinase activity in patient tissue; imaging showing hourglass sites correspond to tunical abnormalities after all; or absence of corporal fibrosis in long-duration patients.

    It would be refuted outright by evidence that the hard flaccid state is not smooth muscle contraction — for instance, direct measurement showing normal cavernosal tone in a symptomatic patient.

    13. Closing

    None of this is knowledge. It is a structured set of guesses arranged so that they can be attacked efficiently. If quantitative sensory testing against matched controls returns a negative result, Conjectures II, IV, XII and XIII lose most of their support. If a Rho-kinase inhibitor fails to alter resting tone, Conjecture V — the strongest here — is finished.

    The field’s problem has never been a shortage of ideas about hard flaccid syndrome. It has been a shortage of ideas stated precisely enough to be wrong. A 2025 systematic review found eight studies meeting its inclusion criteria, and no controlled trial of any intervention has been published.5 Under those conditions the useful contribution is not another explanation. It is a list of experiments ordered by how much they would change the picture, and an honest account of which parts of the current picture are holding it up.

    Important

    This article is speculative hypothesis generation, not medical advice, and not a description of established knowledge. It does not establish a clinician–patient relationship. Nothing in it should be used to select, request or avoid any treatment, and the study designs discussed are analytical reasoning rather than protocols or recommendations. The agents named — including ketamine, α-adrenergic antagonists, Rho-kinase inhibitors, sodium channel blockers and intracavernosal injections — are prescription-only, carry real risks, and are appropriate only under qualified medical supervision. Intracavernosal injection must never be self-administered. Seek emergency care for an erection lasting more than four hours.
